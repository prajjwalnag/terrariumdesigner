    Assembly

    1. Cut. Send the SVG to a laser cutter. Do a test cut on scrap to confirm the kerf and hole sizes.
    2. Print. Print four brackets. The arm length is in the cut list; pockets hold the magnets.
    3. Dry-fit. Stand the four walls on the base and check every edge sits flush before any glue.
    4. Bond. Run thin acrylic cement along each seam in a ventilated room. Hold square until it grabs, then cure for a day.
    5. Magnets. Glue matching magnets into the brackets and under the lid. Test polarity first.
    6. Brackets. Fix one bracket on each top corner so the lid seats on the magnets.
    7. Camera. Mount the ESP32-CAM so the lens looks through its hole. Keep the electronics out of the humid space.
    8. Fill. Add substrate and décor, check for gaps, then house your spider.

    Camera code

    ESP32-CAM sketch

    A starter Arduino sketch for an AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM: a live view page, one-tap snapshots and a video stream you can open on your phone. Put in your Wi-Fi name and password, upload it, and open the address shown in the Serial Monitor.

    Wiring and tips

    • Power from a stable 5 V supply (500 mA or more). Weak power is the most common cause of resets.
    • To upload without a programmer base, use a USB-serial adapter: 5V, GND, U0R to TX, U0T to RX, and tie IO0 to GND while uploading, then remove it and press reset.
    • Keep the board outside the humid space. Only the lens barrel should poke through the 9 mm hole.
    • Wi-Fi stays on the 2.4 GHz band only. Give the camera a fixed address in your router so the link never changes.
    • A DHT22 or BME280 sensor can sit in the printable sensor holder (Toys → Other) next to the camera.

    About the checks

    The fit checks are rules of thumb: a ground dweller wants floor space of about two by one and a half leg spans and a free height of up to one and a half leg spans, while a tree dweller wants at least two leg spans of height and floor space of two leg spans each way. Vent holes are sized to be smaller than the spider can squeeze through. The camera view is based on a typical ESP32-CAM lens (about 52° × 40°).

    Please note: this tool checks geometry only. Humidity, ventilation, escape-proofing, heating and species needs vary. Research your species and ask experienced keepers before building. Cast acrylic and solvent cement need good ventilation; never laser-cut PVC.
